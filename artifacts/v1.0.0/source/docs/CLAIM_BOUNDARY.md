# Claim Boundary

PrimeClock Dynamics v1.0.0 is a public research artifact and preprint-style
manuscript. Its claim surface is the v1.0 fixed-angle Angular Erdos-Kac theorem
program plus a fixed-cutoff CRT uncovered-average identity.

## Claims

This artifact claims:

- a finite-dimensional fixed-angle Angular Erdos-Kac theorem on dyadic windows;
- the one-dimensional fixed-angle diagonal Angular Erdos-Kac theorem as a
  special case;
- the fixed-cutoff complete-CRT Angular CLT input used in the proof;
- slow-cutoff finite-window moment transfer;
- all-angle L1 diagonal tail removal;
- a fixed-cutoff CRT uncovered-average identity;
- the fixed-cutoff uncovered-average asymptotic obtained from the classical
  Mertens product theorem.

The model-internal identity

```text
D(n,0)=omega(n)
```

holds under the documented half-open point-membership convention. The `alpha=0`
fiber recovers the classical prime-divisor count, so the Angular Erdos-Kac
theorem contains the corresponding dyadic `omega(n)` statement as a special
case. This is consistent with the classical Erdos-Kac theorem and is not presented as a new strengthening of the classical theorem.

## Non-Claims

This artifact does not claim:

- uniform-in-alpha convergence;
- a shrinking-angle theorem;
- a log-correlated Gaussian field theorem;
- a full Gaussian field theorem;
- full diagonal moment convergence;
- a diagonal Mertens uncovered-measure law;
- pointwise or typical laws for `U(n)`;
- a new proof of Mertens' theorem;
- a new theorem about prime gaps;
- a result about the Riemann hypothesis;
- a result about classical covering systems;
- a proof of the prime number theorem;
- a theorem extending Prime Reciprocal Covering artifacts;
- a random/non-random theorem;
- peer review.

The fixed-cutoff geometric Mertens identity is a complete-CRT average identity.
It is not a diagonal Mertens law for `U(n)=U_n(n)`.

Field-level extensions beyond a finite fixed set of angles, shrinking-angle
limits, log-correlated field limits, and diagonal Mertens uncovered-measure
statements remain future work.
